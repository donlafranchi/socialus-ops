Trial of the meta layer, framed as tuning. This file covers the score added today; the Friday run adds notes on all scores.

## Is each score measuring what it should? (false reds and misses, with examples)
- `closed_parent_unmet` 16 features, red: **partly false.** 6 of the 16 are scenario Issues in the beta milestone closed with unmet criteria (F081, F082, F099, F101, F102, F080: the real leakage the roll-up audit named). The others are shipped features whose scenario was retired, with criteria reconstructed from git history (F044, F045, F046: 2 to 6 of 5 to 9 met). Those criteria may have been overtaken by newer rulings rather than forgotten. [V from the first reading; the split is an inference to check]
- `untracked_unmet` 21 criteria, red: **mixed.** It counts every unmet criterion with no open Issue in its row. That includes SPEC-CONFLICT criteria, which go to `scope-scenario` by design and have no Issue, and criteria whose row names only a merged PR.
- `unchecked_built_pct` 74.5%, red: **real, but not leakage of a known gap.** It is the share of built criteria with no marked test (105 of 141). It duplicates the roll-up's Checked column.

## What to adjust (threshold, source, formula)
- Split `closed_parent_unmet` into beta-milestone Issues (the PM's concern) and retired-scenario features (informational), the same split the audit makes.
- Exclude SPEC-CONFLICT criteria from `untracked_unmet`, and count them as their own metric once the reports mark the status field.
- Consider dropping `unchecked_built_pct` here and reading it from the roll-up's Checked count instead, so two places do not define it.
- These are changes to a score script, so they go out as `meta-change` PRs the PM approves.
